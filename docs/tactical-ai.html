🧠 Tactical AI

How Creatures Think

Rule-Based Decision Making

Every creature that doesn't have a trained ML model falls back to a rule-based AI that runs through a fixed priority chain each turn. The goal is to approximate competent play — not perfect play — so simulations feel like real encounters rather than random dice rolls.

1
Skip check

If the creature is dead, incapacitated, or has no valid targets, skip the turn immediately.

2
Pick a target

Focus the enemy with the lowest current HP to eliminate threats as fast as possible.

3
Choose a weapon

Pick ranged if the target is far away and the creature has a ranged option; otherwise go melee.

4
Move

Close to melee range if using a melee weapon; maintain kite distance if ranged. Disengage-move if critically low on HP.

5
Attack

Use the main action to attack, fire all Extra Attacks, and burn Action Surge if available and a target is in range.

6
Bonus actions

TWF off-hand attack, Nature's Veil, and other bonus-action abilities fire automatically when conditions are met.

Team Memory

Each team shares a TeamMemory object that tracks live combat state — who is under pressure, which enemies are threatening, and the overall HP ratio across both sides. This state vector is what gets fed into trained ML models each turn.

The memory also drives reactive tactics: if an ally is taking heavy fire, the Protect strategy will route creatures toward that ally rather than chasing the nearest target.

Pluggable Strategies

Any creature can have its behaviour overridden with one of five preset strategies. These can be selected in the Simulator and applied to a whole team:

Aggressive

Close to melee range and attack every turn, ignoring tactical positioning.

Kite

Maintain ranged distance while attacking — stay mobile, avoid melee contact.

Retreat

Flee from the strongest threat. Prioritises survival over damage output.

Focus Fire

Charge the lowest-HP enemy regardless of distance to eliminate targets fast.

Protect

Move adjacent to the most-pressured ally and absorb incoming damage.

ML-trained models replace this strategy selection with a learned policy — see Machine Learning Strategies for how those are trained and deployed.
